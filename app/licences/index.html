The app is made of separately licensed files. Each data file in data/ carries its own licence, and these are the full texts and notices that come with them. Source code and the scripts that build every data file are in the project's repository.

App code

index.html, app.js, core.js, journey.js, today.js, writer.js, style.css, sw.js and data/grammar.json: MIT. Licence text.

vendor/hanzi-writer.min.js: Hanzi Writer 3.7.3 by David Chanin, MIT. Licence text.

Character parts: data/parts.json

Modified from dictionary.txt in Make Me a Hanzi (derived from Unihan and CJKlib), under the GNU Lesser General Public License, version 3 or later. We kept decompositions, hints, definitions and component roles, and reshaped them into JSON. parts.json is itself the modified data, in a plain readable format.

  • Make Me a Hanzi notice
  • GNU LGPL version 3
  • GNU GPL version 3, which the LGPL builds on

Which strokes make which part: data/stroke-parts.json

Derived from the matches field of Make Me a Hanzi's dictionary.txt, under the same GNU LGPL version 3 or later as parts.json (texts above). We kept, for each character, the strokes that belong to each of its top-level parts.

Stroke outlines: strokes/

From graphics.txt in Make Me a Hanzi, derived from the Arphic PL KaitiM GB and Arphic PL UKai fonts, under the Arphic Public License. Changes: split into one file per character, keeping the outlines and centre lines of each stroke. These files are distributed under the same licence.

  • Make Me a Hanzi notice
  • Arphic Public License

Dictionary and frequencies: data/dict.json

Pinyin, English glosses and Traditional forms from CC-CEDICT, and frequency ranks from wordfreq by Robyn Speer (which includes SUBTLEX-CH by Cai and Brysbaert 2010, freely available data, OpenSubtitles and Google Books Ngrams). Both under CC BY-SA 4.0, and this file is shared under the same licence. Changes: entries limited to the items Jiesuo teaches, glosses shortened to their first two senses, pinyin written with tone marks, frequencies given as a weight per million words, and Hong Kong Traditional forms added with OpenCC.

Example sentences: data/sentences.json

From Tatoeba, under CC BY 2.0 FR (some English sentences are CC0). Each row names the author of the Chinese sentence and of its English translation, with their Tatoeba IDs, and the app shows both under every example. Changes: Traditional forms added with OpenCC.

HSK levels: data/hsk.json

From elkmovie/hsk30, MIT, Copyright (c) 2021 Pleco Inc. Licence text.

Learning order: data/order.json

Positions computed by Jiesuo from the sources above. It contains numbers only, no text from them.

Word pictures: pictures/

All emojis designed by OpenMoji, the open-source emoji and icon project, version 17.0.0. License: CC BY-SA 4.0 (full text). The files are unmodified and keep that licence. Which word gets which picture (data/pictures.json) is Jiesuo's own list, MIT.

Fonts

Chinese text uses LXGW WenKai (mainland forms, in Simplified mode) and LXGW WenKai TC (Hong Kong and Taiwan forms, in Traditional mode), by LXGW, under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. They are loaded from jsDelivr and Google Fonts, not shipped here. The app icons are drawn with LXGW WenKai TC.
